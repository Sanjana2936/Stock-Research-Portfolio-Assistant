# Financial Glossary & Educational Material

## Key Ratios
P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings Ratio): The P/E ratio measures a company's current share price relative to its per-share earnings. A high P/E could mean that a stock's price is high relative to earnings and possibly overvalued. Conversely, a low P/E might indicate that the current stock price is low relative to earnings. 

Forward P/E: A version of the ratio of price-to-earnings that uses forecasted earnings for the P/E calculation.

Price-to-Book (P/B) Ratio: Companies use the price-to-book ratio to compare a firm's market capitalization to its book value. It's calculated by dividing the company's stock price per share by its book value per share (BVPS). A P/B ratio under 1 is typically considered a solid investment for value investors.

Debt-to-Equity (D/E) Ratio: Calculated by dividing a company’s total liabilities by its shareholder equity. It is a measure of the degree to which a company is financing its operations through debt versus wholly-owned funds. A high D/E ratio indicates higher risk.

Return on Equity (ROE): A measure of financial performance calculated by dividing net income by shareholders' equity. Because shareholders' equity is equal to a company’s assets minus its debt, ROE is considered the return on net assets.

Profit Margin: Represents what percentage of sales has turned into profits. It is a measure of profitability.

Dividend Yield: A financial ratio that shows how much a company pays out in dividends each year relative to its stock price.

## Risk Metrics
Volatility: A statistical measure of the dispersion of returns for a given security or market index. Higher volatility means higher risk.

Beta: A measure of the volatility, or systematic risk, of a security or portfolio compared to the market as a whole. A beta of 1 indicates that the security's price moves with the market. A beta greater than 1 indicates greater volatility than the market, and less than 1 indicates less volatility.

Portfolio Concentration: Refers to the degree to which a portfolio's assets are invested in a small number of holdings. A highly concentrated portfolio is riskier because a decline in one asset has a larger impact on the overall portfolio value. We use the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) to measure this.

## Regulatory and Educational (SEBI Guidelines)
Retail investors should always verify the source of tips and rumors. SEBI mandates that investment advice should only be given by registered investment advisors (RIAs). 
Investors should diversify their portfolios to mitigate risks.
Do not invest solely based on historical performance, as past performance is not indicative of future results.
Always check the fundamentals of the company before investing.
